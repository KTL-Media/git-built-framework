# GIT INVEST Tesseract Client Workflow Addendum

## Purpose

Add a productized AI video-editing service track to GIT INVEST. Participants use Tesseract locally with an AI coding or desktop agent to create short-form edits, hook variations, motion-graphics launch videos, and monthly content retainers.

The operating principle is simple: sell finished deliverables, not hours. The client is buying a usable video and a reliable turnaround, not the time spent operating the tool.

## The client workflow

### 1. Qualify

Look for clients who already have content but lack a dependable editing system:

- Local businesses posting static images or uncaptained video
- Podcasters and YouTubers with long-form content but few short clips
- Founders preparing a product launch
- Coaches, artists, educators, and creators who need recurring social content

Ask:

- Do you already have footage?
- What platform are you publishing on?
- How often do you need videos?
- What outcome matters: reach, leads, sales, launch awareness, or consistency?
- Who approves the final edit?

### 2. Diagnose the content gap

Classify the opportunity:

| Client situation | Recommended offer |
|---|---|
| One good clip needs polish | Single Edit |
| Client needs to test different openings | Hook Pack |
| Client has an upcoming release or launch | Launch Video |
| Client publishes weekly or has a content library | Monthly Retainer |

### 3. Produce proof before selling

Create three samples using footage you shot yourself, free stock, or footage for which you have permission:

1. Talking-head clip with captions and a strong hook
2. Product promo with an animated title
3. One clip with four alternate openings

Keep the files organized as reusable portfolio assets. Do not post client or third-party footage publicly without permission.

### 4. Deliver through a controlled revision loop

Use this standard loop for every client:

1. Create a first cut
2. Show a preview
3. Collect one consolidated note
4. Revise
5. Show the second preview
6. Render and quality-check the final file

The agent drafts. The operator approves. Human review remains the quality-control layer.

## Productized offers

| Offer | Scope | Price guidance | Delivery |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Single Edit | One short-form video up to 60 seconds; captions, music, clean cuts, animated title; two revision rounds | $75–$100 | 48 hours |
| Hook Pack | One video plus four alternate openings for testing | $200 | 48–72 hours |
| Launch Video | 30–60 second product or launch video with motion graphics, kinetic type, and logo end card | $300–$750 | 2–5 days |
| Monthly Retainer | 12 short-form videos per month, captions and hooks included, one revision round each | $900–$1,500/month | 3 per week |

These are market-range starting points, not guaranteed income. Adjust for complexity, client value, rights, rush timing, and the quality of your portfolio.

## Commercial terms

- Collect 100% upfront for a first job with a new client.
- Collect 50% upfront on projects over $100 once trust is established.
- Include two revision rounds.
- Charge $25 for each additional revision round.
- Charge a 50% rush premium for same-day delivery.
- Use client-provided or properly licensed music.
- Review every render before delivery.
- Define what is included, what counts as a new project, and the response time for retainers.

## Seven-day client acquisition sprint

### Day 1 Build proof

Finish the three portfolio samples. Create a one-page offer sheet with links, turnaround, revision policy, and starting prices.

### Day 2 Submit targeted proposals

Search freelance marketplaces for short-form editing jobs and apply only where your samples match the requested style.

Proposal structure:

> Hi [name], I watched the reference videos in your post. The [specific detail] is exactly the style I work in. Here are three relevant samples: [links]. For this project I would deliver [X] videos with captions, music, and a strong opening within [turnaround]. Two revision rounds are included. I can also provide four alternate openings for the first video so you can test which hook performs best. Would you like me to start with one video so you can judge the quality first?

### Day 3 Contact local businesses

Find businesses that already publish but do not use captions, hooks, or motion graphics.

DM structure:

> Hey [name], I like what you are doing with [business]. I noticed your videos do not have captions yet, and many people watch Reels on mute. I edit short-form videos for local businesses. If you send me one clip you already filmed, I will edit it and send it back this week so you can see the difference. If you like it, videos start at $75 each.

Only make a free sample when the prospect is qualified and the scope is limited.

### Day 4 Contact podcasters and creators

Look for long-form content with obvious clip moments but little short-form distribution.

DM structure:

> Hey [name], your episode with [guest] had a moment at [timestamp] that could work well as a Reel. I edit short-form clips for podcasts. Would you like me to cut three clips from your latest episode? If they are not useful, you owe me nothing. If they are, I can create clips from every episode for a monthly price.

### Day 5 Contact founders

Find founders announcing launches on X, LinkedIn, Product Hunt, or community channels.

DM structure:

> Hey [name], congratulations on the upcoming launch of [product]. I make launch videos for founders: 30–60 seconds, motion graphics, kinetic type, and a logo end card. If you send me a screen recording of the product, I can show you a first version within 48 hours. Launch videos start at $300.

### Days 6 and 7 Follow up and close

Follow up once after 48 hours:

> Hey [name], just floating this back up. I can still do that first sample edit this week if you want to see the difference. No pressure either way.

When a prospect likes the sample, give two choices:

> Glad you like it. I have two ways to continue: $75 per video as you go, or 12 videos per month for $900 with captions and hooks included. Most clients start with four videos to test the workflow. Which option fits better?

## First-client success metric

The target for the first week is one paid job, not ten clients. One paid job validates the offer. After that, increase the volume of qualified proposals, sample edits, follow-ups, and upsells.

## Use-case menu for the workshop

Teach participants to choose one lane first:

1. **Local business content engine** — turn phone footage into captioned Reels and Shorts.
2. **Podcast clip factory** — turn each episode into three to twelve short clips.
3. **Founder launch studio** — turn screen recordings into polished launch videos.
4. **Hook testing service** — create multiple openings and help clients identify stronger creative.
5. **Creator retainer** — deliver a predictable monthly short-form publishing package.
6. **Agency white-label editing** — provide fast production capacity behind an agency’s client relationships.

## Workshop integration

Add this as a practical service track inside GIT INVEST:

### Session 1 Install and first proof

- Verify Mac or Windows compatibility
- Verify local agent access and Node.js/npm
- Install Tesseract using the official Mirage repo or approved plugin/skills path
- Create the first 10-second test
- Practice preview, note, revision, and render

### Session 2 Offer and pricing

- Choose one client lane
- Build the three-sample portfolio
- Select a productized offer
- Create a price sheet and terms
- Define the revision and rush policy

### Session 3 Distribution and outreach

- Build a 25-lead list
- Personalize proposals and DMs
- Send the first outreach batch
- Produce one qualified free sample
- Track every response

### Session 4 Delivery and recurring revenue

- Deliver the first paid job
- Collect a testimonial or permission to use the sample
- Offer a Hook Pack or monthly retainer
- Document the workflow as an SOP
- Review margins and improve the offer

## Risk controls

- Use only footage you own or have permission to edit.
- Use licensed music or client-supplied music; do not pull tracks from Spotify.
- Keep client files in the correct project folder and do not expose private footage.
- Review every AI-assisted output before delivery.
- Do not promise performance results unless the contract clearly defines the measurement and dependency.
- Keep the first offer narrow so free samples do not become unpaid production work.
