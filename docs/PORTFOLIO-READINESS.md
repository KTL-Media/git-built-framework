## Portfolio presentation
Use three front doors: Education & Curriculum; Content Production & Implementation; Software Experiments. Each project needs a short problem statement, intended buyer, current screenshot or demo, exact setup instructions, honest status, named maintainer, license/rights position, and a next milestone with measurable acceptance criteria.

Do not count the same curriculum or customer twice across projects. Decks and plans are assets; they do not prove a functioning application. Promotional revenue targets are not historical revenue.

## Buyer and funder fit
Education: paid cohorts, institutional delivery, sponsored seats, and partner-led digital literacy proposals.
Content production: customer-funded pilots and retainers.
Public framework: licensed public reuse and agreed implementation partnerships; private access does not convey ownership.
Software experiments: bootstrap validation first. Consider venture funding only after evidence of repeat usage, retention, scalable economics, differentiation, and rights.

These are candidate lanes, not verified awards, open solicitations, contracts, or investor interest.

## Proof packet for each opportunity
- Product evidence: reproducible setup, demo recording, sample output, known limitations.
- Customer evidence: permissioned references, paid invoice evidence, usage and renewal records.
- Economics: revenue definition, direct delivery and API costs, support hours, gross margin calculation.
- Rights: contributor permissions, source/media licenses, ownership, permitted commercial use.
- Milestone: what the next budget buys, who owns delivery, when it is due, and how acceptance is measured.

Keep client data and confidential financial evidence private. Publish only approved summaries.

## First delivery priorities
Content Engine: repair CLI instructions and enforce input-bound publishing approval; then demonstrate an owned recording through rendering.
Foundry: distinguish generated ideas from rendered clips and leave human review pending; then prove a real provider run.
KLAWS and Imaginarium: curate one current deliverable per offer and test it with a learner or independent facilitator.
Student Workbench and Source Pack: resolve exact aliases and commercial roles before presenting them as separately owned products.

## Completion bar
A repository is ready for a prospect when someone else can understand it, run or use its actual deliverable, see evidence of its value, and identify what remains unfinished. Investment readiness additionally requires validated economics and ownership, not cosmetic polish.
